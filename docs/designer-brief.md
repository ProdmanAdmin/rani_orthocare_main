## 2. The business {#business}

### What the clinic believes (the core values)
These come from the doctor's own voice note and drive everything on the site.

1. **Relief first.** A patient in pain gets relief before anything else.
2. **Find the real cause.** Then the clinic finds and treats why it hurts, not only the pain.
3. **Movement is the goal.** Patients who come in on a stretcher or in a wheelchair should walk out.
4. **Options before surgery.** Non-surgical care, physiotherapy and injection treatments (PRP, GFC, BMAC) are considered before an operation.
5. **Prevention for working people.** Desk workers, riders, drivers and manual workers are taught how to avoid neck and back pain, including sessions at companies.
6. **Safety first.** Every treatment must be safe.
7. **Care for the community.** Free medical camps.

### What the clinic offers (confirmed by the doctor)

| Area | Details |
|---|---|
| Everyday orthopaedics | Fractures, knee, shoulder and joint pain, back and neck pain, arthritis, ligament and sports injuries, children's bone problems (such as clubfoot) |
| At the clinic | Specialist consultation, digital X-ray, plaster and dressing, minor surgery in the clinic's own **Mini OT** |
| Tests and injection treatments | Bone marrow aspiration cytology (a test); PRP, GFC and BMAC (Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate) injections |
| Major surgery | Knee and hip replacement and spine surgery, done at **tie-up hospitals** |
| Physiotherapy | Given at a **separate, dedicated centre**, not at the clinic |
| Emergency care | Available 24/7 (how night calls work is still to confirm) |
| Free camps | A free bone density (BMD) screening camp every 3 months, plus several small camps every month |
| Hours | Monday to Saturday 9 AM to 10 PM, Sunday 10 AM to 12 PM (always as on Google) |

### The doctor (the face of the brand)
- **Training:** MBBS from Tirunelveli Medical College; MS (Ortho) from Government Rajaji Medical College, Madurai.
- **Posts:** Consultant Orthopaedic Surgeon; also Assistant Surgeon at a Government Hospital.
- **Experience:** 10 years.
- **Local roots:** born in Karaikudi and schooled at SMSV Higher Secondary School there. This is a strong local trust point.
- **Photo:** a profile photo is coming. Until real photos arrive, design with clearly marked placeholders. Don't use stock photos of "doctors".

### What makes this clinic different
Most orthopaedic websites lead with surgery. This one leads with **relief and movement**, with surgery as one option among several. The distinct stories are:
- options before surgery
- prevention for working people
- free camps for the community

## 3. Who the site is for {#audience}

| Who | What they need from the site |
|---|---|
| **Older adults** with knee, back or joint pain (the main audience) | Large, clear text; obvious buttons; reassurance; the phone number |
| **Families** of patients (children booking for parents, parents for babies) | Quick facts: what is treated, where, when, how to book |
| **Working people:** desk and IT workers, riders, drivers, manual workers | Neck and back pain help, prevention, timings that fit work |
| **People told they need surgery** | Calm explanation of other options, and honesty about when surgery is right |
| **Companies** | A way to ask about posture sessions at their workplace |

- **Where they are:** the brand area is about 15 to 20 km around the clinic. Patients come from up to 70 to 80 km away.
- **What they read:** many are biliterate (Tamil and English), and some read one language much better than the other.
- **What they use:** design for phones first, including low-cost phones with small screens.

**Design implication:** clarity beats style. If a visual idea makes text harder to read for a 65-year-old on a cheap phone, it loses.

## 4. How we arrived at the message {#journey}

The strategy was built in steps. Each step was reviewed with the project lead before the next.

| Step | What we did | What came out of it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Listen to the doctor | His voice note in Tamil describing the business, transcribed and turned into a clean English and Tamil version | The business model and the core values (section 2) |
| 2. Gather the facts | Logos, visiting cards and print materials; the Google Business Profile; Instagram | Confirmed name, address, phone, hours (from Google) and services. Unconfirmed sources were ruled out |
| 3. Study the competition | Seven reference orthopaedic websites, every page | What works (clear service tiles, doctor-led trust, sticky call buttons) and the gap: they all lead with surgery |
| 4. Choose the message framework | StoryBrand: the patient is the hero, the clinic is the guide | One story for the whole site (section 5) |
| 5. Design system | Fonts, colours, type sizes and components for older, bilingual readers | `DESIGN.md` and the mood board in the prototype |
| 6. Fix the Tamil | The first Tamil was too formal, textbook Tamil. We agreed **Style A: simple written Tamil**, using the English words people actually say (அப்பாயின்ட்மென்ட், டாக்டர், ஆபரேஷன்) | The Tamil style guide; all Tamil rewritten and approved on a home page review |
| 7. Ask the doctor | A questionnaire on his profile, area, camps, emergency care, surgery and physiotherapy | His answers changed the copy. Physiotherapy is not at the clinic, so it is no longer called a "physiotherapy clinic". Minor surgery happens in the clinic's Mini OT, major surgery at tie-up hospitals. The camps became their own section |
| 8. Write the home page | Every section in page order, in both languages, on the StoryBrand framework | The **Home Page Content** document and PDF |

**Why StoryBrand:** a visitor in pain is not interested in the clinic's story; they are interested in their own. StoryBrand makes every page answer three questions in a few seconds:
- What is my problem?
- How can you help?
- What do I do next?

It also gives the designer a clear job for every section.

## 5. The StoryBrand framework, in short {#storybrand}

| Part | For Rani Ortho Care |
|---|---|
| **Hero** | The patient: someone in pain who wants to walk, work and live their day again |
| **Villain** | Pain that keeps you from moving (never another doctor or clinic) |
| **Problem** | External: pain, stiffness, injury. Internal: worry, depending on family, fear that surgery is the only option. Philosophical: everyone deserves to move without pain |
| **Guide** | Dr. N. Manikandan. Empathy: "We know pain makes every step heavy. We start by easing it." Authority: training, 10 years of experience, local roots, Google rating 4.9 |
| **Plan** | 3 steps: 1. Call or WhatsApp to book. 2. See the doctor: pain first, then the cause. 3. Move again with a clear plan |
| **Call to action** | Direct: Book an appointment · Call · WhatsApp. Transitional: find your problem, ask on WhatsApp, get directions, camp updates |
| **Stakes** | Said gently, once per page: pain left alone can make daily life harder; many problems are simpler to treat early |
| **Success** | "Back to the things you love: a morning walk, a full day's work, time with your family." |

Full framework, with every line in English and Tamil: `docs/storybrand.md`.

## 7. Content placement principles {#placement}

1. **The main action is never more than one tap away.** Book or Call appears in the header, the hero, after key sections, and in the phone action bar.
2. **Relief before surgery, visually too.** Surgery appears as one option among several, never as the hero image or the first tile.
3. **Proof sits next to claims.** Put the trust strip right after the hero, and the Google rating right after the doctor.
4. **One idea per section.** If a section needs two headings, it is two sections.
5. **Never two bands of the same colour next to each other.** Use the band order in section 6.
6. **Tamil is not a translation layer.** Tamil text is often much longer than the English. Every heading, card and button must hold longer Tamil without breaking:
   - headings may wrap to 2 or 3 lines
   - buttons may wrap to 2 lines
7. **Facts come from one place.** Phone, hours and address always match Google. Don't type them differently in a design.
8. **Placeholders are obvious.** Until real photos arrive, use clearly marked placeholders, not stock photos.
9. **Show where things happen.** Patients should never be surprised: X-ray and minor surgery at the clinic, major surgery at tie-up hospitals, physiotherapy at a separate centre.

## 8. Design system: what is already decided {#design}

Full detail: `DESIGN.md` (version 1.2). The visual mood board is section 4 of the prototype.

- **Fonts:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for English, made for low-vision readers. Noto Sans Tamil for Tamil.
- **Colours:**
  - navy `#14234A` for headings and the footer
  - brand blue `#3C59A8`
  - action green `#1B7030` for buttons
  - the logo's spine green `#3DB54A` for decoration only, because it is too light for text
  - background bands: white, sky `#E8F0FA`, green `#EAF5EC` and navy
- **Type sizes:**
  - body text 18 px on phones and 20 px on desktop
  - H1 28, 34 and 40 px (phone, tablet, desktop)
  - Tamil gets taller line heights
- **Buttons:** at least 56 px tall. A glossy green gradient for the main button, with gentle hover and press effects.
- **Effects:** soft gradients, frosted glass cards and light shadows. All motion switches off for people who ask for reduced motion.
- **Screens:** phones under 640 px, tablets 640 to 1023 px, desktops from 1024 px. Content width up to 1200 px.
- **Accessibility:** text contrast at least 4.5:1, tap targets at least 44 px, visible keyboard focus, and every image described.

## 9. Rules that are not negotiable {#rules}

**Medical advertising (India):**
- no "best", "No. 1", "leading", "guaranteed", "cure" or "permanent"
- no fixed recovery times
- no comparisons with other doctors
- no before/after photos or patient testimonials without the doctor's approval

**Accuracy:**
- The town name appears once per page in the body text.
- Hours always follow Google.
- Nothing is invented: if a fact isn't confirmed, it isn't on the site.
- **Physiotherapy is not at the clinic:** never call it a "physiotherapy clinic".

**Wording and language:**
- No long dashes in website copy.
- Tamil follows Style A, simple written Tamil: see `docs/tamil-style-guide.md`.

**Search (SEO):**
- One H1 per page.
- Headings in order (H1, then H2, then H3). Card titles are not headings.
- These matter for search and AI answer engines, so the visual hierarchy should match.
